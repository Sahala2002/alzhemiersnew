MEDICAL INFORMATION

About Alzheimer’s Disease

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurological disorder that primarily affects memory, thinking, behavior, and the ability to perform everyday activities.

Definition

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and one of the most common causes of dementia. It gradually affects memory, thinking, communication, reasoning, and the ability to perform everyday activities.

As the disease progresses, changes in the brain can lead to increasing difficulties with cognitive and daily functioning.

Causes

The exact cause of Alzheimer’s disease is unknown. However, several biological, genetic, and environmental factors are associated with its development and progression.

01

Exact Cause is Unknown

The exact combination of factors responsible for Alzheimer’s disease has not been completely established.

02

Abnormal β-Amyloid Accumulation

Abnormal accumulation of β-amyloid protein can form plaques in the brain and interfere with normal neuronal communication.

03

Tau Protein Abnormalities

Abnormal changes in tau protein can contribute to the formation of neurofibrillary tangles inside neurons.

04

Genetic Factors

Certain genetic factors can increase the risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease.

05

Aging-Related Neuronal Changes

Changes that occur in neurons and brain function with aging may contribute to Alzheimer’s disease.

06

Neuroinflammation and Neuronal Damage

Inflammation in the brain and progressive neuronal damage may contribute to cognitive decline.

DISEASE PROCESS

Pathophysiology

The progression of Alzheimer’s disease involves a series of biological changes that gradually affect neuronal function and cognitive abilities.

PROGRESSION

Types & Stages

Alzheimer’s disease progresses gradually and symptoms generally become more noticeable over time. The condition can be described using different stages based on cognitive and functional changes.

01

Early Stage

Mild memory problems, difficulty finding words, reduced concentration, and difficulty with some complex everyday activities may occur.

02

Middle Stage

Memory and language difficulties become more noticeable. Assistance with everyday activities may be required, and behavioral changes can occur.

03

Late Stage

Severe cognitive impairment may occur, with increasing dependence on caregivers for communication, personal care, and daily activities.

CLINICAL FEATURES

Signs & Symptoms

Common signs and symptoms associated with Alzheimer’s disease include the following.

01

Memory Loss

Increasing difficulty remembering recently learned information, conversations, appointments, or important events.

02

Confusion

A person may become confused about dates, places, familiar surroundings, or situations.

03

Difficulty Performing Familiar Tasks

Everyday activities that were previously familiar may become increasingly difficult to complete.

04

Language Problems

Individuals may have difficulty finding the correct words, following conversations, or expressing their thoughts clearly.

05

Personality & Behavior Changes

Changes in mood, personality, social behavior, or emotional responses may occur as the disease progresses.

06

Problems With Judgment

Difficulty making decisions, solving problems, or evaluating everyday situations may develop.
