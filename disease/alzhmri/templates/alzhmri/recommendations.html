Alzheimer's Disease Care
& Recommendations

Practical guidance for supporting the safety, health, independence, and quality of life of people living with Alzheimer's disease.

CARE GUIDANCE

Supporting Daily Life with Alzheimer's Disease

Good care involves medical follow-up, a safe environment, healthy lifestyle habits, meaningful activities, and supportive communication.

01
💊

Medication Adherence

  • Take prescribed medicines regularly and at the correct time.
  • Do not stop medicines without medical advice.
  • Report unusual or adverse effects to the healthcare provider.
02
🩺

Regular Medical Follow-up

  • Attend scheduled medical appointments.
  • Cognitive function, behavior, nutrition, and general health can be monitored.
  • Report sudden worsening of confusion, falls, fever, or significant behavioral changes.
03
🕐

Maintain a Daily Routine

  • Maintain regular waking, meal, bathing, activity, and sleep times.
  • Use calendars and clocks to provide orientation.
  • Labels and reminder notes can help with everyday tasks.
04
🏠

Safety Precautions

  • Keep floors clear and provide adequate lighting to reduce fall risks.
  • Secure medicines, chemicals, and dangerous objects.
  • Consider an identification card or medical-alert bracelet if wandering is a concern.
  • Driving safety should be discussed with the healthcare team.
05
🥗

Healthy Diet & Hydration

  • Encourage a balanced diet containing protein, fruits, vegetables, and whole grains.
  • Encourage adequate fluid intake unless a healthcare professional has prescribed fluid restriction.
  • Monitor for chewing, swallowing, or feeding difficulties.
06
🚶

Physical Activity

  • Encourage safe and regular physical activity according to the person's ability.
  • Walking and other simple exercises can be useful options.
  • Encourage activities that the person enjoys and can perform safely.
07
🧩

Cognitive & Social Activities

  • Encourage conversation, music, reading, puzzles, gardening, or familiar hobbies.
  • Social interaction can provide emotional support and meaningful engagement.
  • Activities should be adapted to avoid frustration and excessive fatigue.
08
🌙

Sleep Management

  • Maintain a regular sleep and wake schedule.
  • Encourage daytime activity and exposure to natural light.
  • Avoid excessive daytime sleeping when possible.
09
💬

Communication

  • Speak slowly and use short, simple sentences.
  • Ask one question at a time.
  • Give the person enough time to respond.
  • Avoid arguing or repeatedly correcting the person.
⚕

Important Medical Note

This information is intended for general health education and does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Alzheimer's disease affects individuals differently, so care plans should be individualized with guidance from qualified healthcare professionals.
